Best Base Layers of 2026

By GearGrid Staff October 8, 2026 3 min read

A good base layer is the foundation of staying warm in winter. Its job isn't just insulation — it moves sweat away from your skin so you don't get chilled the moment you stop moving. That's why merino wool has become the go-to fabric for serious cold-weather layering.

Merino is soft, regulates temperature well, and resists odor far better than synthetics, so you can wear it for multiple days on a trip. We compared fabric weight, fit, and whether each set comes as a matching top and bottom.

What to Look For

  • Fabric weight: Around 150–200 gsm is lightweight for active use; 250 gsm and up is midweight-to-heavy for cold, low-output days.
  • Merino content: 100% merino maximizes warmth and odor resistance; blends add durability and stretch.
  • Fit: Base layers should sit close to the skin to wick moisture — snug, but not restrictive.
  • Flatlock seams: Flat seams prevent chafing under packs and ski boots.
  • Care: Most merino is machine washable on a gentle, cool cycle; skip fabric softener.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is merino wool itchy?

Modern merino uses very fine fibers that most people find soft against skin. If you're sensitive, a merino blend is usually even gentler.

Should base layers be tight?

They should be close-fitting so they can wick moisture, but not so tight that they restrict movement or circulation.

Can you wear a base layer alone?

Yes, around the house or on mild days. For real cold, add an insulating mid layer and a wind- or water-resistant shell.

How do you wash merino base layers?

Machine wash cold on a gentle or wool cycle, skip fabric softener, and lay flat or hang to dry to keep the fit.
