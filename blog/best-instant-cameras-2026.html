Best Instant Cameras in 2026

GearGrid Staff June 3, 2026 8 min read

Instant cameras have staged a remarkable comeback — and not just as nostalgia objects. In 2026, shooting on film and holding a physical print seconds later offers something that digital photography genuinely can't: a tangible, shareable artifact that doesn't disappear into a phone's camera roll. Instant cameras are genuinely popular gifts and event accessories. The market has matured into clear tiers, each doing something different well.

We evaluated instant cameras on print quality, film cost, ease of use, special features, and price. These four picks cover every use case from the simplest point-and-shoot to creative control for photography enthusiasts.

Our Top Picks

#1 — Fujifilm Instax Mini 12: Best Overall

The Fujifilm Instax Mini 12 at around $80 is the best instant camera for most buyers. Its Automatic Exposure control reads ambient light and adjusts shutter speed to produce correctly exposed prints in far more conditions than earlier Instax Mini models could handle. Close-up mode (engaging the built-in selfie mirror and adjusting the lens) lets you shoot portraits and detail shots at arm's length without overexposure. Five colors available.

Instax Mini film is the most widely available instant film in the world — two-packs at roughly $16 for 20 shots are found at pharmacies, big-box stores, and online. The credit-card-sized 62×46mm prints are easy to carry, stick to walls, and share. For a first instant camera or a gift, the Instax Mini 12 is the most reliable, consistent, and easy-to-use option available.

#2 — Fujifilm Instax Mini 11: Best Value

The Fujifilm Instax Mini 11 at around $70 is the slightly older sibling to the Mini 12, and still an excellent buy. Auto Exposure came to Instax Mini with the 11 (the first in the line to have it), selfie mode works well, and the pastel color palette is popular. The 11 lacks the close-up macro improvement of the 12 and one or two minor ergonomic refinements, but for most users the real-world difference is minimal. The $10 savings means two more packs of film.

If the Mini 12 is sold out in the color you want or on sale, the Mini 11 is a completely valid alternative. They use identical film, have comparable print quality, and require identical levels of photographic skill (none).

#3 — Fujifilm Instax Wide 300: Best for Bigger Prints

The Fujifilm Instax Wide 300 at around $100 shoots the same instant film technology as the Mini cameras but outputs prints nearly twice the size: 99×62mm (roughly a standard postcard). When you want your instant photos to actually be visible across a table, framed on a wall, or usable as event mementos rather than tiny wallet photos, the Wide 300 is the answer. It also includes a parallax viewfinder correction, landscape and portrait modes, and a usable close-up setting.

Wide film costs more per shot than Mini (~$18 for 20 shots vs $16 for 20 Mini shots) but the prints are proportionally more impactful. The Wide 300 is the pick for party hosts, event photographers, and anyone who's found Mini prints too small for their use case.

#4 — Polaroid Now+ Gen 2: Best for Creative Control

The Polaroid Now+ Gen 2 at around $130 is for people who want to actually control their instant photography rather than just point and shoot. Bluetooth connectivity pairs with the Polaroid app to give you manual mode (adjust aperture, shutter speed, double exposure), five creative lens filters (starburst, bokeh, light painting, self-timer, remote shutter), and an interface for experimentation. Polaroid's i-Type and 600 film formats produce that distinctive square format with the wide white border that defines Polaroid's aesthetic.

Polaroid film costs more per shot than Instax (~$18 for 8 shots vs $16 for 20 Instax Mini shots), which is a real consideration for frequent use. But the artistic possibilities — double exposures, long exposures, the unique look of Polaroid emulsion — are genuinely different from Instax. For photography enthusiasts who want an instant camera as a creative tool, not just a party accessory, the Now+ Gen 2 is the pick.

What to Look For

Film Costs Over Time

The camera purchase is just the start — factor in ongoing film costs. Instax Mini (20 shots ~$16) is the most economical at $0.80/shot. Polaroid i-Type (8 shots ~$18) runs $2.25/shot. If you're buying a camera for a child or heavy event use, the difference between formats compounds quickly over time. Fujifilm's Instax film is also far more widely available in stores, making impulse buys and last-minute restocking easier.

✓ Our Verdict

Best Overall: Fujifilm Instax Mini 12

For most buyers, the Instax Mini 12 at $80 is the right answer — auto exposure, close-up mode, and the most widely available film in the world. On a tight budget? The Instax Mini 11 at $70 uses the same film and delivers comparable prints. Want bigger prints for events or wall displays? The Instax Wide 300 at $100 is the upgrade. Creative photographer who wants actual control? The Polaroid Now+ Gen 2's manual mode and app integration are worth the higher film cost.
