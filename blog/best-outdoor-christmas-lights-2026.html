Best Outdoor Christmas Lights of 2026

By GearGrid Staff October 8, 2026 3 min read

Outdoor Christmas lights have to deal with rain, snow, and long hours switched on, so it pays to choose sets built for it. Modern LEDs use a fraction of the power of old incandescent strands and stay cool to the touch.

We compared waterproof ratings, length, connectability, and bulb styles, from fairy-light strings for trees to classic C9 bulbs for rooflines.

What to Look For

  • Waterproof rating: IP44 or higher handles rain and snow; check the plug and controller too.
  • Length and connectability: End-to-end plugs let you link sets to cover long rooflines from a single outlet.
  • Bulb style: Mini or fairy LEDs suit trees and bushes; big C9 bulbs give the classic roofline look.
  • Modes and timers: Built-in timers turn lights on at dusk automatically and save energy.
  • Color: Warm white looks traditional; multicolor reads as festive and playful.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can indoor Christmas lights be used outside?

Not safely. Outdoor-rated lights have weather-resistant wiring and plugs; indoor lights aren't built for moisture.

How many light strands can I connect?

It depends on the set. LED strands can usually connect many more than incandescent ones; check the maximum on the box.

Do LED Christmas lights save money?

Yes. LEDs use far less electricity than incandescent bulbs and last much longer.

How do I hang lights on a roofline?

Use plastic gutter or shingle clips rather than nails or staples, which can damage the wires and your roof.
