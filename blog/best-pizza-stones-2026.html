Best Pizza Stones in 2026: Crispy Crusts from Your Home Oven

By GearGrid Staff July 23, 2026 8 min read

A pizza stone (or steel) solves the core problem with home oven pizza: a standard baking sheet doesn't conduct heat fast enough, so the bottom crust steams instead of crisps. A preheated stone or steel transfers heat directly to the dough the instant it lands, producing a restaurant-quality crispy bottom in the same 8–12 minutes a home oven allows.

Here are the best pizza stones of 2026 — cordierite stone, ceramic, and steel options evaluated for thermal mass, temperature tolerance, and how quickly they recover heat between pizzas.

What to Look For

  • Stone vs. steel: Steel conducts heat 20x faster than stone and gets hotter faster — it's the better tool for crispy thin-crust and Neapolitan-style pizza. Stone has more thermal mass, holds heat longer, and is better for thick-crust and focaccia. Steel is more expensive but more durable.
  • Cordierite vs. ceramic: Cordierite is heat-shock resistant up to 2000°F — it can go from cold oven to broil setting without cracking. Ceramic stones are cheaper but crack if thermally shocked. Always preheat cordierite gradually (45–60 min at 500°F).
  • Thickness: Thicker stones and steels hold more thermal energy and recover faster between pizzas. 3/8" steel is the minimum; 1/2" is better. For stone, 5/8"+ holds heat better than thin 1/2" stones.
  • Size: Measure your oven rack before buying. Most home ovens accommodate a 15" round or 14x16" rectangular stone. Leave 1" clearance on all sides for air circulation.
  • Seasoning and care: Never soap a pizza stone — it absorbs water and can crack when heated. Scrape debris, brush clean. Steel needs occasional re-seasoning (thin coat of oil, heat in oven at 450°F for 1 hour).
