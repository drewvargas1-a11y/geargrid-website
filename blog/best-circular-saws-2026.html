Best Circular Saws in 2026: Clean Cuts on Every Job

By GearGrid Staff July 23, 2026 8 min read

A circular saw is the most versatile cutting tool in any shop or job site — it cuts framing lumber, plywood sheets, decking, and trim faster and more accurately than a hand saw, and handles jobs that a miter saw or table saw can't reach. For any DIYer doing more than occasional projects, it's the first power tool worth owning.

Here are the best circular saws of 2026 — corded for raw sustained power and cordless for portability — evaluated by cut quality, bevel capacity, blade visibility, and whether they're actually comfortable to control through a full sheet of plywood.

What to Look For

  • Corded vs. cordless: Corded saws deliver consistent power regardless of battery state — better for long production days and thick material. Cordless saws (20V MAX, 18V) match corded performance for most tasks and are significantly more convenient. A 18V/20V brushless cordless saw with a 5Ah battery is the current recommendation for most DIYers.
  • Blade size: 7-1/4" is the standard — it cuts up to 2-9/16" at 90° (through 2x framing lumber). Smaller blades (6-1/2") are lighter but cut shallower. Larger blades (8-1/4") are rare for hand-held saws — usually specialized for framing.
  • Blade left vs. right: Left-blade saws (blade on left) give right-handed users a direct sight line to the cut line. Right-blade saws (traditional) require looking over the motor. Left-blade is ergonomically superior for right-handed users — worth seeking specifically.
  • Bevel capacity: Saws bevel 45–56° for angled cuts. A 56° bevel (instead of the standard 45°) allows compound miter cuts that 45° saws can't make. Essential for roofing and angled trim work.
  • Anti-snag lower guard: Cheap saws have lower guards that catch on the workpiece when starting cuts at an angle. Better guards retract smoothly at any angle. Test this specifically when demoing a saw — it's the most frequent irritation in daily use.
