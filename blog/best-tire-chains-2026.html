Best Tire Chains of 2026

By GearGrid Staff October 19, 2026 3 min read

Tire chains turn a slippery climb into a manageable one, and in many mountain areas they're legally required when chain controls go up. Today's options range from traditional link chains to low-clearance cable chains and fabric snow socks.

Fit is everything: chains are sold by tire size, so start with the size printed on your sidewall and check your owner's manual for clearance limits. We compared ease of installation, clearance, and where each type works best.

What to Look For

  • Exact tire size: Every set lists the tire sizes it fits. Match it to your sidewall (for example 225/65R17).
  • Wheel-well clearance: Many modern cars have tight clearance; low-profile cable chains are designed for them.
  • Installation style: Self-tensioning and front-mount designs install faster and without driving onto the chain.
  • Local rules: Some states and passes require chains or approved alternatives; check before you travel.
  • Speed limit: Most chains are rated for about 30 mph. Remove them on bare pavement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need chains on all four tires?

Most cars need chains on the drive wheels only. Check your owner's manual; many all-wheel-drive vehicles specify which axle.

Can I use tire chains on dry pavement?

No. Chains wear quickly and can damage the road and your car on bare pavement. Remove them as soon as conditions allow.

Are snow socks legal instead of chains?

In many U.S. areas they are accepted, but rules vary by state and pass. Check the local transportation department before relying on them.

How fast can I drive with chains?

Usually no more than about 30 mph. Follow the manufacturer's limit and posted signs.
