Best OBD2 Scanners of 2026

By GearGrid Staff July 10, 2026 8 min read

An OBD2 scanner plugs into the diagnostic port under your dashboard (every car since 1996 has one) and reads the codes that cause a check engine light — letting you diagnose issues yourself before spending $150+ at a dealership for the same information.

From sub-$25 Bluetooth adapters that work with your phone to professional-grade scanners with live data and bi-directional controls, these are the best OBD2 scanners in 2026.

What to Look For

  • Basic vs. Advanced: Basic scanners read and clear diagnostic trouble codes (DTCs). Advanced scanners add live sensor data, O2 sensor tests, EVAP system testing, and bi-directional controls for actuator testing.
  • Bluetooth vs. Handheld: Bluetooth adapters pair with free or premium smartphone apps — low cost and convenient. Dedicated handheld scanners need no phone and are faster to use in the driveway.
  • Coverage: Ensure the scanner covers your vehicle's make and year. Most OBD2 scanners cover generic powertrain codes. Manufacturer-specific codes require more advanced scanners.
  • App Quality: For Bluetooth adapters, the companion app is everything. Free apps like Torque Pro provide impressive data; manufacturer apps vary widely.
  • Live Data: The ability to view real-time sensor data (coolant temp, O2 sensor voltage, fuel trims) is far more valuable than just reading codes for diagnosing intermittent issues.
