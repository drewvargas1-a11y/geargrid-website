Best Photo Printers in 2026: Print Memories at Home

By GearGrid Staff July 23, 2026 8 min read

A dedicated photo printer produces prints that smartphone labs can't replicate — you control the color profile, paper choice, and finish, with no waiting for shipping or driving to a store. Modern inkjet photo printers produce gallery-quality prints that last 100+ years, and dye-sub printers produce smudge-proof, waterproof prints instantly.

Here are the best photo printers of 2026 — evaluated for print quality at 4x6 and 8x10 sizes, ink cost per print, photo-specific paper handling, and how they compare to professional lab output.

What to Look For

  • Inkjet vs. dye-sublimation: Inkjet: highest quality, wide paper sizes (up to 13x19" on pro models), but ink costs add up. Dye-sub: instant prints, waterproof immediately, smudge-proof, but limited to 4x6" or 5x7". For wall prints and archival quality — inkjet. For quick snapshots and parties — dye-sub.
  • Ink cost per print: Photo ink is expensive. Calculate cost per 4x6 print: inkjet typically $0.15–0.40 per print including paper. Dye-sub typically $0.30–0.50 per print (all-inclusive refill kits). Compare to lab printing ($0.10–0.30 at Walmart/CVS) when buying.
  • Color accuracy: 6-ink systems (CMYK + light cyan + light magenta) produce smoother skin tones and gradients than 4-ink CMYK. Pigment inks last longer archivally (100+ years). Dye inks (more common) produce slightly more vibrant colors but fade faster without proper storage.
  • Print longevity: Pigment-based inkjet (Epson UltraChrome) prints last 100–200 years under glass. Dye-based prints last 25–60 years. Dye-sub prints are waterproof but fade faster than pigment inkjet in UV light.
  • Connectivity: WiFi printing from your phone is the baseline expectation now. AirPrint (iPhone) and Mopria (Android) are standard. USB direct print from a camera SD card is useful for photographers.
