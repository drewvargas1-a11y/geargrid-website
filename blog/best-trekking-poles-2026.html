Best Trekking Poles in 2026: More Miles with Less Fatigue

By GearGrid Staff July 19, 2026 8 min read

Trekking poles reduce impact on the knees by up to 25% on descents — a significant number for anyone with existing knee issues or hiking steep terrain. They also improve balance on loose rock and stream crossings, and can be used as tent poles for ultralight shelter setups.

Here are the best trekking poles of 2026, ranked by weight, collapse mechanism (fold-down vs. telescoping), grip material, and shock absorption for different terrain types.

What to Look For

  • Carbon vs. aluminum: Carbon fiber is lighter (20–30% weight savings) and absorbs trail vibration better. Aluminum is more durable — bends rather than snapping under sudden lateral force. For rough rocky terrain, aluminum is safer.
  • Locking mechanism: Twist locks are the lightest but can loosen over time. Lever locks (flick locks) are more reliable and easier to adjust with gloves. Fold-down poles use a cord system — fastest to deploy.
  • Grip material: Cork grips absorb sweat and mold to hand shape over time — best for hot climates. Foam grips are lightweight and warm. Rubber grips are the most durable but get hot in summer.
  • Tip types: Carbide tips provide traction on hard surfaces and rock. Rubber tip covers protect surfaces in national parks, campgrounds, and urban sections. Baskets (wide discs near tip) prevent sinking in snow and mud.
  • Anti-shock: Spring-loaded anti-shock systems dampen vibration on hard terrain. Add slight weight and cost but make a noticeable difference on all-day rocky hikes. Skip it for lightweight backpacking.
