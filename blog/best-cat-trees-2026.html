Best Cat Trees in 2026: Towers, Scratching Posts, and Condos Ranked

By GearGrid Staff July 19, 2026 8 min read

Cats need vertical space to climb, scratch, and survey their territory. Without it, they redirect those instincts to your furniture and walls. A cat tree that reaches near the ceiling satisfies the climbing urge, provides a scratching outlet that saves your couch, and gives cats a territory they can actually own.

Here are the best cat trees of 2026, ranked by stability (the critical factor), scratching post durability, platform count, and overall build quality across price ranges.

What to Look For

  • Height and stability: Taller trees wobble more easily — critical for larger cats. Look for heavy bases, wall-mounting options, and reviews from owners with large or active cats.
  • Sisal coverage: Natural sisal rope scratching posts are durable and cats prefer the texture. More coverage means more surfaces to scratch. Avoid carpet-covered posts — they last half as long.
  • Perch size: Perches under 12" wide are fine for kittens and small cats. Large cats (over 12 lbs) need 15"+ platforms to sit comfortably without hanging off the edge.
  • Condo placement: Condos (enclosed boxes) at mid-height give shy cats a hide spot. Ground-level cubbies work better for older cats that can't jump as high.
  • Multi-cat households: Add 1 tree per cat ideally, or buy a tree with at least as many perches as you have cats so they're not competing for the same spot.
