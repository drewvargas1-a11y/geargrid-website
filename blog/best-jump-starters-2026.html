Best Jump Starters in 2026

By GearGrid Staff August 28, 2026 8 min read

A dead battery is the most common roadside emergency — and a portable jump starter means you never need to wait for another car or a tow truck. Modern lithium-ion jump starters pack 1,000–2,000 peak amps into a unit that fits in a glove box, replaces a traditional lead-acid jump pack at 1/5th the weight, and doubles as a power bank for USB devices.

We evaluated peak current output, cold cranking amps, engine size compatibility, safety features, battery capacity, and real-world reliability to find the best jump starters for every driver.

What to Look For

  • Peak amps vs. cranking amps: Peak amps is the maximum burst the unit can deliver — cranking amps is the sustained output needed to actually start an engine. Most lithium starters list peak amps prominently; look for units with at least 400 cranking amps for reliable starts on larger engines.
  • Engine size compatibility: A 1,000A unit handles up to 6.0L gas and 3.0L diesel. A 2,000A unit handles up to 8.0L gas and 6.0L diesel — necessary for trucks, SUVs, and European diesel engines.
  • Safety protection: Reverse polarity protection (to prevent sparks if clamps are accidentally reversed) and overload protection are non-negotiable. NOCO's UltraSafe technology is the industry standard.
  • Cold weather performance: Lithium batteries lose capacity in extreme cold. Units rated for use down to -4°F (-20°C) will still work in winter conditions; cheaper lithium cells may fail to deliver enough current below freezing.
  • Built-in flashlight: Useful but not essential. A strong LED flashlight on the unit is genuinely helpful for dark parking garages and nighttime breakdowns.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a lithium jump starter hold its charge?

Quality lithium jump starters like the NOCO GB40 hold their charge for 12–18 months in storage with minimal self-discharge. Lead-acid units like the JNC660 need to be recharged every 30–90 days to prevent sulfation. Check the manufacturer's storage recommendations and recharge before winter storage.

Can a jump starter charge a completely dead battery?

A jump starter provides the burst current needed to start the engine — it doesn't charge the battery. After jump-starting, run the engine for at least 20–30 minutes to let the alternator recharge the battery. If the battery dies again quickly, it needs replacement, not just another jump.

What's the difference between peak amps and cranking amps?

Peak amps is the maximum current the unit can deliver for a fraction of a second. Cranking amps is the sustained current delivered for 30 seconds at 32°F — the real-world number that determines whether it can start your engine. A unit with 1,000 peak amps but only 200 cranking amps may struggle on larger engines in cold weather.
