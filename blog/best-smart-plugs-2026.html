Best Smart Plugs for Your Home in 2026

GearGrid Staff May 30, 2026 8 min read

Smart plugs are the easiest entry point into home automation. They require no wiring, no electrician, and no technical knowledge — you plug them into an existing outlet, connect them to Wi-Fi, and any dumb lamp, fan, or appliance instantly becomes controllable from your phone, by voice, or on a schedule. The right smart plug can automate your morning routine, cut standby power consumption, or give you peace of mind that you turned off the coffee maker before leaving for work.

We evaluated four of the best smart plugs in 2026 based on reliability, app quality, energy monitoring capability, smart home platform support, and physical size — since some plugs block the second outlet on a duplex. These picks range from $10 to $22 and cover indoor, outdoor, and energy-monitoring use cases.

Our Top Picks

#1 — Kasa KP115: Best Overall

The Kasa KP115 at around $20 is the best smart plug for most homes. It combines a slim profile that doesn't block the second outlet, built-in energy monitoring that tracks real-time wattage and monthly kWh usage, and Kasa's consistently excellent app — one of the most reliable and intuitive in the smart home category. The KP115 supports Amazon Alexa, Google Home, and Samsung SmartThings. It does not require a hub.

The energy monitoring is the feature that elevates the KP115 above competitors. Being able to see that a specific appliance is drawing 180W constantly, or that your "standby" device is actually consuming 12W at all times, enables real decisions about energy use. Kasa's scheduling and automation tools are mature and work reliably — set schedules once and they run without maintenance. For a single best pick that covers nearly all indoor use cases, the Kasa KP115 is the answer.

What to Look For

Smart Home Platform Compatibility

The single most important decision when buying a smart plug is which smart home platform(s) you use. Alexa and Google Home compatibility is nearly universal — almost every smart plug supports both. Apple HomeKit is the exception: far fewer devices natively support it, and it matters if you want Siri control or to use Apple's Home app as your hub. Matter-compatible smart plugs are also appearing, which promise universal compatibility across all major platforms — worth looking for if long-term flexibility is a priority.

Energy Monitoring

Smart plugs with energy monitoring (like the Kasa KP115) track both real-time power draw in watts and cumulative consumption in kilowatt-hours. This turns a simple on/off switch into a tool for understanding which devices in your home actually consume significant electricity — information that can identify energy hogs and validate whether unplugging standby devices actually saves meaningful power. If you care about your electricity bill, energy monitoring pays for itself quickly.

Physical Size and Outlet Blocking

Not all smart plugs are created equal in size. Some bulky designs completely block the second outlet in a standard duplex outlet, making them effectively a single-plug solution. Before buying, check whether the plug has a pass-through design or side-oriented plug that preserves access to both outlets. The Kasa KP115 and Meross MSS110HK are both slim enough to leave the second outlet usable — an important practical consideration if you're plugging into a shared outlet location.
