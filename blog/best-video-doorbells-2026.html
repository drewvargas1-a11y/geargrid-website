Best Video Doorbells in 2026

GearGrid Staff June 3, 2026 8 min read

A video doorbell is one of the highest-value smart home upgrades you can make. It tells you who's at the door before you open it, records package deliveries and porch pirates, and lets you answer the door from anywhere in the world. In 2026, even budget video doorbells deliver 1080p HD video, night vision, and two-way audio. The meaningful differences are in subscription costs, response time, field of view, and smart home integration.

We evaluated four video doorbells on video quality, response latency, cloud vs local storage, subscription requirements, and smart ecosystem compatibility. These picks range from $65 to $150.

Our Top Picks

#1 — Ring Video Doorbell 4: Best Overall

The Ring Video Doorbell 4 at around $100 is the best all-around video doorbell for most homes. 1080p HD video with HDR, color pre-roll (4 seconds of color video before motion is detected — so you see the whole approach, not just the moment it triggers), improved motion zones, and Alexa integration make this the most complete package at $100. Battery-powered means no wiring required — a 10-minute setup in most homes.

Ring's Basic plan at $4/month stores video history for 180 days. The Ring ecosystem means it works seamlessly with Echo Show displays (your doorbell video shows on your Echo screen automatically when someone rings). Two-way audio is clear. Night vision is solid in color with the pre-roll feature. If you're in the Amazon/Alexa ecosystem and want the most polished experience at this price, the Ring Video Doorbell 4 is the pick.

#2 — eufy Security S220 SoloCam: Best No-Subscription

The eufy Security S220 at around $100 is the pick for anyone who refuses to pay a monthly subscription. It stores 30 days of video locally on the doorbell itself — no cloud, no monthly fee, no account dependency. 2K resolution is sharper than Ring's 1080p. AI-based person detection reduces false alerts from cars and animals. Two-way audio with noise cancellation. Battery lasts 4–6 months on a charge.

The eufy app is clean and responsive. Local storage means your footage is never dependent on a server staying up. The trade-off is that the eufy ecosystem is smaller than Ring's — no smart display integration as polished as Ring + Echo. For privacy-focused buyers and anyone who wants great video quality without ongoing fees, the eufy S220 is the clear choice.

#3 — Ring Video Doorbell (2nd Gen): Best Budget

The Ring Video Doorbell (2nd Gen) at around $65 is the most affordable capable video doorbell available. 1080p HD video, night vision, motion detection, two-way audio, and Alexa integration — all the essentials at the lowest price in this category. Battery-powered for easy installation. A good entry point for first-time buyers who want to try a video doorbell before committing to a full smart home camera system.

The main limitation vs the Ring 4 is the absence of color pre-roll and HDR. Motion detection is good but not as nuanced. For a budget-conscious buyer or a rental property, the 2nd Gen Ring delivers 90% of the value at 65% of the price of the Ring 4.

#4 — Google Nest Doorbell (Wired, 2nd Gen): Best Premium

The Google Nest Doorbell (Wired, 2nd Gen) at around $150 is the premium choice, particularly for Google Home users. Continuous video recording (wired connection means no battery limitation), 3:4 head-to-toe aspect ratio that captures package delivery at your feet and your visitor's face simultaneously, on-device processing for faster alerts, and deep Google Home integration make this the most capable doorbell in this roundup. Works with Google Assistant and Nest displays.

Google Home's Nest Aware plan at $8/month stores 60 days of event history and 10 days of continuous recording. The wired requirement means more setup complexity — but eliminates battery anxiety entirely. For Google Home households who want the absolute best video quality and feature set, the Nest Doorbell Wired 2nd Gen delivers it.

What to Look For

Wired vs Battery

Battery-powered doorbells are easier to install — no electrician needed — but require charging every few months. Wired doorbells have constant power for continuous recording and never need charging, but require existing doorbell wiring or professional installation. If you have existing doorbell wiring, go wired. If not, battery is the practical choice.

Subscription Costs

Most video doorbells work without a subscription — you get live view and real-time alerts. Subscriptions unlock video history storage. Ring's Basic plan at $4/month is among the most affordable. eufy's local storage eliminates subscriptions entirely. Factor the lifetime subscription cost into your decision — over 3 years, Ring's plan adds $144 to the total cost.
