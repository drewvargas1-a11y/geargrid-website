Best Gaming Controllers in 2026

By GearGrid Staff June 26, 2026 8 min read

The controller market has diversified dramatically — you're no longer locked to the manufacturer's default option. Hall effect thumbsticks that never drift, back paddle buttons, adjustable trigger stops, and remappable layouts are now available from $50 to $250. In 2026 the best controllers in each tier rival professional esports peripherals from just a few years ago.

We evaluated ergonomics, input latency, build quality, customization options, and platform compatibility across these four picks.

What to Look For

  • Hall effect sticks: Magnetic hall effect sensors don't develop thumbstick drift because there's no physical contact between the magnet and sensor. 8BitDo and some Gulikit controllers use these. Worth seeking out if drift has been a past problem.
  • Back paddles: Extra buttons on the back of the controller — remappable to any function — let you keep thumbs on the sticks during complex inputs. Standard on elite models, optional on mid-range.
  • Trigger stops: Hair-trigger toggles shorten the trigger travel distance for faster fire rate in shooters. Useful for competitive FPS players.
  • Wireless latency: 2.4GHz wireless is faster than Bluetooth for gaming. Xbox Elite 2 and Razer Wolverine use proprietary 2.4GHz adapters for near-wired latency.
  • Battery life: Rechargeable battery packs (USB-C or proprietary) are now standard on premium controllers. AA battery designs like standard Xbox controllers allow hot-swapping.
