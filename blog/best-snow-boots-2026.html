Best Snow Boots of 2026

By GearGrid Staff October 19, 2026 3 min read

A good snow boot keeps your feet dry, warm, and steady on slick ground. That means a waterproof lower, real insulation, a tall enough shaft to keep snow out, and a lugged rubber sole that grips packed snow and ice.

We compared insulation, waterproof construction, shaft height, and traction across men's and women's boots, from heavy-duty pac boots to lighter everyday styles.

What to Look For

  • Waterproof construction: Look for a rubber lower or seam-sealed upper. Listings sometimes contradict themselves, so check the specific model and colorway.
  • Insulation: 200g synthetic insulation is common for active use; removable felt liners add warmth for very cold, still conditions.
  • Temperature rating: Brands quote ratings such as -25°F or -40°F. Treat them as relative comparisons rather than guarantees.
  • Shaft height: Taller boots keep deep snow out; ankle-height boots are lighter for errands and commuting.
  • Outsole: Deep lugs and soft rubber compounds grip better on packed snow and cold pavement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How warm should snow boots be?

For active use like shoveling, 200g insulation is usually plenty. For standing still in very cold weather, a pac boot with a removable liner is warmer.

Should snow boots be a size bigger?

Many people go up a half size to leave room for thick socks, but check each brand's sizing notes. Too much room lets your foot slide and lose heat.

Are snow boots good on ice?

Lugged rubber soles grip packed snow well, but no boot is truly safe on glare ice. Slip-on ice cleats add traction when you need it.

How do you dry snow boots?

Remove the liners and insoles if possible and dry at room temperature. Avoid direct heat from radiators or hair dryers, which can damage leather and glue.
