Best Budget Smartwatch 2026: Top Picks Under $100 (Tested)

GearGrid Staff May 14, 2026 7 min read

Three years ago, a "budget smartwatch" meant a $20 plastic slab with a display that washed out in sunlight and step counts that were more fiction than fact. That era is over. In 2026, the best budget smartwatches under $100 are legitimately good — AMOLED panels, continuous heart rate monitoring, built-in GPS, and week-long battery life. The gap between a $400 Apple Watch and a $60 Amazfit has narrowed more than most people realize.

We spent time testing budget smartwatches across different price points, tracking their GPS accuracy against dedicated running watches, comparing heart rate data against chest-strap monitors, and living with them daily. Here's what we found.

What to Look For in a Budget Smartwatch

Before you buy, there are four things that actually matter:

  • AMOLED vs LCD: AMOLED displays are sharper, brighter in sunlight, and more power-efficient. At the $50–$100 range, most good watches now offer AMOLED. If a watch has an LCD at this price, skip it.
  • Built-in GPS vs connected GPS: Built-in GPS tracks your runs and hikes independently from your phone. Connected GPS uses your phone's signal — it works, but you must carry your phone. Under $60, connected GPS is common. Over $70, most options have built-in.
  • Battery life: Look for at least 7 days in regular use. The best budget watches hit 10–14 days. Always-on display cuts battery in half, so plan accordingly.
  • Water resistance: 5 ATM or higher means you can swim with it. Anything labeled "IP67" or "splash resistant" means avoid water. Look for the ATM rating.

Our Top Budget Smartwatch Picks for 2026

#1 — Amazfit Bip 6: Best Overall Under $100

The Amazfit Bip 6 is the clear winner at the $79 price point. It packs a crisp 1.97-inch AMOLED display that's readable in direct sunlight, accurate GPS with dual-band support, continuous heart rate monitoring, blood oxygen tracking, and a 10-day battery life in normal use. The Zepp app has improved significantly and now provides genuinely useful sleep analysis and recovery scores. It's compatible with both iPhone and Android, and the wrist-detection for screen wake is reliable enough that you won't miss a notification.

If you're buying your first smartwatch or looking to replace an aging Fitbit, this is the one to get.

#5 — Fitbit Inspire 3: Best for Health Tracking

Fitbit's Inspire 3 is the best option if health data is your primary goal. Fitbit's sleep tracking is still the most detailed in the budget category — Sleep Score, sleep stages, and HRV are all included. You also get stress tracking, active zone minutes, and a 10-day battery. The 1.0-inch color display is smaller than the Amazfit options, and there's no GPS (it uses connected GPS from your phone), but the companion app and Fitbit Premium integration are unmatched if you want long-term health trend data. At around $79, it's a strong choice for health-focused buyers.

Who Should Skip a Budget Smartwatch

Budget smartwatches are excellent for casual fitness tracking, notification management, and daily health awareness. They fall short if you're a serious runner who needs route maps on the wrist, a cyclist tracking power output, or someone who needs ECG readings for medical purposes. For those use cases, look at the Garmin Instinct 2 Solar or a mid-tier Apple Watch.
