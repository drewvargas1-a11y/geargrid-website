Best Wireless Earbuds Under $100 in 2026

GearGrid Staff May 16, 2026 7 min read

You don't need to spend $200+ to get great wireless earbuds in 2026. The sub-$100 market has matured to the point where active noise cancellation, 30-hour battery life, and excellent sound quality are all attainable without premium pricing. The challenge is cutting through the noise — there are hundreds of options, and many of them aren't worth your time.

We've researched across expert reviews from RTINGS, What Hi-Fi, and SoundGuys, cross-referenced with thousands of real-world owner reviews. These four picks represent the best value at every price point in the budget earbud category.

1. Anker Soundcore Liberty 4 NC — Best Overall

The Anker Soundcore Liberty 4 NC is the benchmark for sub-$100 earbuds and it's not particularly close. At around $80, you get active noise cancellation that legitimately competes with earbuds at twice the price, 10mm drivers that produce a full, balanced sound signature, and up to 28 hours of total battery with the case. The LDAC codec support means Android users get high-resolution audio streaming, something you'd normally pay $150+ for.

ANC performance is the headline — real-world tests consistently rank it among the best ANC under $100, blocking out low-frequency rumble (planes, AC units, traffic) effectively. The companion app lets you adjust EQ and ANC intensity. Call quality is good but not exceptional. If you want one pair of earbuds under $100 that does everything well, this is it.

2. JLab Go Air Pop — Best Budget Pick

At just $25, the JLab Go Air Pop shouldn't sound this good. They're not going to compete with the Soundcore Liberty 4 NC on noise cancellation (there isn't any), but for pure wireless convenience at a rock-bottom price, these are genuinely impressive. Sound is V-shaped — boosted bass and treble — which most casual listeners enjoy. Battery life is solid at 32 hours total, and the charging case is small enough to forget about.

The Go Air Pop excels at exactly what budget earbuds should: working reliably, being comfortable for hours, and not making you feel like you wasted money. They're the answer for gym use, mowing the lawn, or a pair to keep in your bag as a backup. Don't expect detail retrieval or ANC — expect a fun, stress-free listen at a price that doesn't sting if you lose them.

3. Sony WF-C700N — Best Sound Quality

Sony's WF-C700N sits right at the $100 mark and brings Sony's renowned audio tuning to the budget tier. The sound signature is more neutral and accurate than most earbuds at this price — midrange clarity is noticeably better, which makes vocals and acoustic instruments sound more lifelike. ANC is present and competent, though not quite at the level of the Soundcore Liberty 4 NC. The Speak-to-Chat feature (auto-pauses music when you talk) is a genuinely useful daily driver feature.

Battery life is the one weakness at around 15 hours total with the case — below average for the price. But if you care about how your music actually sounds more than any other spec, the Sony's audio quality is a clear step above everything else in this roundup.

4. Jabra Elite 4 — Best for Calls

If you're on calls constantly — work-from-home meetings, commuting phone calls, Teams and Zoom — the Jabra Elite 4 is built for you. Jabra's microphone technology is industry-leading in this price range. Four-mic setup with wind noise reduction means callers on the other end can actually understand you clearly, even in noisy environments. That's not true of most budget earbuds.

Sound quality for music is good but not the best in this group. ANC is present and works well in most environments. Battery is strong at 28 hours total. The Elite 4 also supports multipoint connection — paired to two devices simultaneously — which is rare under $100 and essential if you switch between a laptop and phone constantly.
