Best Spark Plugs in 2026

By GearGrid Staff August 28, 2026 8 min read

Spark plugs ignite the air-fuel mixture in each cylinder — worn plugs cause misfires, poor fuel economy, rough idle, and hard starting. Modern iridium and platinum plugs last 60,000–100,000 miles, but worn plugs are one of the most commonly neglected maintenance items. The electrode material determines both performance and longevity.

We evaluated electrode material (iridium vs. platinum vs. copper), firing tip design, heat range, anti-seize coating, and compatibility with direct-injection and turbocharged engines to find the best spark plugs of 2026.

What to Look For

  • Iridium vs. platinum vs. copper: Iridium has the highest melting point and smallest electrode tip (0.4–0.6mm) for the most precise spark — the best choice for modern engines. Double-platinum is the OEM standard for most 2005–2015 vehicles. Copper plugs require replacement every 20,000–30,000 miles but deliver the most voltage in older high-compression engines.
  • OEM match: Your engine is tuned for a specific plug gap, heat range, and electrode style. Using the OEM-specified plug or an exact-match replacement from NGK, Denso, or ACDelco is always safer than a generic plug with incorrect specs.
  • Heat range: Stock heat range (as specified for your vehicle) is correct for street use. Going one range colder is only appropriate for modified engines running higher boost or nitrous. Running too cold a plug in a stock engine causes fouling.
  • Pre-gapped: Most modern iridium plugs come pre-gapped to a standard measurement. Always verify the gap against your service manual — some applications require adjustment. Do not close an iridium or platinum tip by force; the fine tip breaks easily.
  • Anti-seize: Quality plugs are coated to prevent seizing in the cylinder head threads. Never apply additional anti-seize to iridium or platinum plugs — it changes the torque-to-clamping relationship and can cause the plug to back out.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know when spark plugs need replacement?

Check your owner's manual for the recommended interval — typically 30,000 miles for copper, 60,000 for platinum, and 100,000 for iridium. Symptoms of worn plugs include: engine misfires (check engine light with P030x code), rough idle, poor acceleration, increased fuel consumption, and difficulty starting. Modern vehicles with direct injection can also develop carbon deposits on plug tips that require earlier replacement.

Can I use iridium plugs in an older engine?

Yes — iridium plugs work in any gasoline engine as long as the thread size, reach, hex size, and heat range match the OEM specification. In older engines designed for copper plugs, you'll notice improved throttle response and better fuel economy. Use the correct part number from NGK or Denso's fitment database, not a generic iridium plug.

Do I need to use anti-seize on spark plugs?

Most modern iridium and platinum plugs have a factory-applied nickel anti-seize coating on the threads — do not add additional anti-seize. Adding anti-seize changes the torque relationship and can result in over-torquing, which strips threads or cracks the ceramic insulator. Torque plugs to spec using a torque wrench.
